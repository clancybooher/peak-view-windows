Window Replacement in Redmond, Oregon

Redmond sits on more open ground than most of Bend. Wind, grit, and a big 1990s–2000s housing wave are what actually drive replacement here — not a copy of Bend's pine-covered neighborhoods.

Serving Redmond

Built for Redmond's Wind and Growth-Era Homes

Redmond sits roughly 600 feet lower than Bend and takes more sustained wind on the flats around Dry Canyon, the airport corridor, and the older neighborhoods west of Highway 97. A sash that is merely inefficient in a sheltered Bend yard becomes a dust-and-draft problem once it has sat in that wind for twenty years.

A large share of Redmond's housing went up between the 1990s and mid-2000s with builder-grade dual-pane glass. Those seals are now at or past typical service life. Fogged corners, sluggish cranks, and a draft you can feel at the sash are the usual first signs — not a Bend-style log-home or historic-trim story.

Oregon's residential code still requires a U-factor of 0.27 or lower in this climate zone, same as Bend. We quote Redmond the same way we quote Bend: free in-home measurement, climate-rated Andersen 100 Series for most openings, and a firm itemized number before any work starts. CCB #260230.
