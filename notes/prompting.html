Prompting like a person, not a prompt pack

You do not need a library of magic phrases. You need a clear job.

Most prompting advice is a pile of tricks. "Act as a senior whatever." "Think step by step." I skip that.

I write prompts the way I write a note to a new hire. What is the job. What does good look like. What do you never do. Then I watch the first week of work.

Three lines, then examples

Start with three lines.

  • Job: draft a reply to this email in my voice.
  • Voice: short. First name. No sales language. I sign Clancy.
  • Stop: save a draft. Do not send.

Then paste two or three of your real replies. That does more than any adjective. The model copies how you actually write, not how a brochure thinks a contractor writes.

A prompt I would actually use

Something like this, in your words:

"You write follow-up emails for my shop. Read the thread. Draft two short paragraphs. Offer a time if they asked for one. If they are mad, stop and tag me. Save the draft. Do not send."

That is a prompt. It has a job, a voice, and a line it cannot cross. You can read it out loud. If you cannot, it is too cute.

When it is wrong

Do not sit there rewriting the output every time. Fix the prompt. Add one line: "Never say excited. Never offer a discount I did not write." Run it again on the same email.

If it still misses, the job is too big. Split it. One agent drafts. You send. Another agent can wait for a later week.

What I do with you

We sit on a screen share. We take five real emails or five real call notes. We write the prompt together. We watch what comes back. We change one thing at a time.

You leave knowing how to give the agent a job in Claude, ChatGPT, Grok, Hermes, or Claude Cowork. You do not leave with a binder of prompts you will never open.
