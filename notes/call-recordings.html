Recorded calls to next steps

The call already happened. The write-up should not take twenty minutes.

You hang up. You mean to type the note. A truck pulls up. The note never lands. Next week nobody remembers if she wanted Thursday or Friday.

Quo already kept the recording. A lot of plans will even write a short summary. Useful. The part that still slips is the CRM note and the text that should go out. That is the job I wire. You still approve send.

What the agent does

Call ends. Claude (or ChatGPT, if that is the home we picked) listens. It writes a short CRM note. What they asked. What you said. The open item. Then it drafts a follow-up text with the times you actually offered.

The text sits in Quo as a draft. You send it or you don't. That is the stop.

What it does not do

It does not book the job. It does not invent a price. It does not text a customer who sounded mad. Those go to you with a tag.

If the recording is junk, it says so. It does not guess.

Why this is a good first agent

The trigger is clean. A call ends or it does not. The output is a note you can read in ten seconds. You can tell in a week if it is helping.

It also teaches the rest of the setup. Once you have seen a stop point work on a real call, wiring the CRM or Gmail makes more sense. Same pattern. Different door.

How we set it up

Screen share. I connect Quo to the model. We run last week's recordings through it. We fix the prompt until the notes sound like you. Then we turn it on for new calls only.

You keep Quo. You keep the model account. I write down the steps so you can change the wording later without me.
