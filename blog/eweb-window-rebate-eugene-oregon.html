EWEB Window Rebate Guide for Eugene Homes

Who qualifies for the EWEB window rebate in Eugene, what the $4 and $20 per square foot rebates require, and what does not qualify.

If you live in Eugene and EWEB is your electric utility, you may be able to get money back for replacing old windows. It is one of the few window rebates left in Oregon. Energy Trust of Oregon ended its Oregon residential window incentives at the start of 2026, and the federal 25C window tax credit expired at the end of 2025.

The EWEB rebate has clear rules, and plenty of homes do not qualify. Here is how it works, in plain terms, so you know where you stand before anyone quotes you.

Programs change. Everything below reflects EWEB's published program as of fall 2026. Confirm the current rules with EWEB before you order windows.

Who Qualifies

To qualify for the EWEB window rebate, all of these need to be true:

  • You are an EWEB customer. The account holder, the owner, or someone authorized on the account signs the application.
  • Your home is heated mainly with electricity. EWEB requires primary electric heat serving at least 50% of the home. Electric baseboard, an electric furnace, and a heat pump are typical examples. A home heated mainly with gas or wood does not qualify.
  • Your existing windows are single-pane or metal-frame double-pane. This is the rule that rules out the most homes. Replacing double-pane wood or vinyl windows does not qualify.
  • The new windows meet EWEB's efficiency rating. For the standard rebate, that means a U-factor of 0.22 or lower (glass patio doors at U-0.25 or lower).

The rebate is figured on the square footage of glass, so a house with a lot of big single-pane windows sees more back than a house with a few small ones.

EWEB also offers zero-interest loans for qualifying window projects, based on your credit history with EWEB. For the standard program, you choose either the rebate or the loan, not both. Income-qualified applicants can combine a rebate and a loan. EWEB publishes the income limits on its website.

What Does Not Qualify

EWEB lists these as not eligible:

  • Replacing existing double-pane wood or vinyl windows.
  • Converting a flat window to a garden window.
  • Making a window opening larger, unless it is for a bedroom egress window.
  • Windows in garages or other unheated spaces.

If your house is heated mainly with gas, or your windows are already double-pane vinyl, the EWEB window rebate will not apply. That does not mean replacing them is a bad idea, only that the rebate is not part of the picture.

How the Process Works

  1. Check your heat and your windows. Electric heat as the main system, and single-pane or metal-frame windows. If both are true, keep going.
  2. Get a quote that lists the U-factor for every window. EWEB needs documentation of each window's rating, such as the window stickers or the manufacturer's order confirmation. Our quotes list the U-factor for every opening.
  3. Talk to EWEB before you order if you are income-qualified. EWEB's income-qualified program has its own steps, and it is worth calling them first.
  4. Install. We install, and we keep the window labels and order paperwork EWEB asks for.
  5. Submit the paperwork. EWEB must receive the installation documentation before it processes any payment.

The U-0.22 Question

The standard rebate's U-0.22 requirement is stricter than Oregon code, which calls for U-0.27 or lower. Not every window line or configuration reaches U-0.22, and the rating can change with the glass package and the window style. A quote that says "energy efficient" without a number is not enough here.

If the rebate matters to you, tell us when we come out. We will check the rating on every window we quote and tell you plainly whether it qualifies, so you are not counting on money that will not come.

What About Springfield and the Rest of Lane County?

The EWEB rebate is for EWEB customers. Springfield is served by the Springfield Utility Board, and areas around Eugene are served by other utilities such as EPUD and Lane Electric, each with its own programs. In Douglas County, Roseburg is mostly Pacific Power, where the Energy Trust window incentive has ended. If you are not on EWEB, check with your own utility.

Worth Doing Even Without a Rebate?

Often, yes. Single-pane and old aluminum windows are usually the coldest, wettest, and least comfortable surfaces in a Western Oregon house. We cover the bigger picture in why new windows pay off in Roseburg and Eugene and in our guide to replacing single-pane windows in older homes.
