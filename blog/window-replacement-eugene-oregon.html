Window Replacement in Eugene, Oregon

Window replacement in Eugene, Oregon: rain, moss, hot west-facing rooms, the EWEB window rebate, and what a good replacement fixes in older homes.

Window replacement in Eugene is mostly about water and sun. From October into spring, rain runs down the glass for weeks at a time. Then summer turns warm and dry, and the west side of the house bakes through the late afternoon. Older windows struggle with both, and plenty of Eugene homes still have the windows they were built with.

What Eugene Weather Does to Windows

Eugene sits in climate zone 4C, the marine zone. The winters are mild by temperature and hard by moisture, and that shapes how windows fail here:

  • Rot and soft sills. Wood sills and lower sash rails that stay wet all winter go soft. Moss and mildew on the north side of the house, especially under big trees, are a sign that side never fully dries out.
  • Failed flashing. Water that gets past an old window often shows up as staining under the window inside, or soft siding and trim below it outside.
  • Inside condensation. A house closed up for the wet season holds a lot of moisture, and single-pane or aluminum windows are the coldest surface in the room, so they sweat. Over the years that water damages sills and trim.
  • Hot west rooms. On summer afternoons, west-facing glass lets in a lot of heat. Low-E glass cuts much of that.
  • Smoke. In bad smoke years, tight-sealing windows keep more of it outside.

What We See in Eugene Homes

Eugene has a wide range of houses: older homes near downtown and around the university, post-war and mid-century neighborhoods, 1970s and 1980s split-levels, and hillside homes in the South Hills with big view windows. Many still have single-pane wood windows or aluminum frames from the 1960s and 70s, and a lot of early double-pane windows have lost their seals.

There are also a lot of rentals in Eugene. Landlords replacing windows tend to ask the same things homeowners do: what will hold up, what is easy to maintain, and whether EWEB will help.

The EWEB Window Rebate

If EWEB is your electric utility and your home is heated mainly with electricity, you may qualify for EWEB's window rebate. The standard rebate is $4 per square foot of glass when you replace single-pane or metal-frame windows with high-performance windows rated U-0.22 or lower. Income-qualified homeowners replacing single-pane windows can receive up to $20 per square foot. EWEB also offers zero-interest loans for windows.

Not every project qualifies. Replacing double-pane wood or vinyl windows does not, and neither do windows in garages or unheated spaces. Springfield and the areas outside Eugene are served by other utilities with their own programs, and Energy Trust of Oregon ended its Oregon window incentives at the start of 2026.

We cover the details in our EWEB window rebate guide. Program rules change, so confirm with EWEB before you order. When we quote, we will tell you whether the windows we are recommending meet EWEB's requirements.

What We Recommend

For most Eugene homes we recommend the Andersen 100 Series. Its Fibrex composite frame does not rot, swell, or need paint, it is rated twice as strong as vinyl, and the color runs through the material. It meets the 2023 Oregon code's U-0.27 requirement. EWEB's standard rebate asks for more, U-0.22 or lower, so if the rebate matters to you, we will check the rating on every window we quote and tell you plainly whether it qualifies.

When you want a real wood interior, we step up to the Andersen 400 Series. When quality vinyl is the better fit for your house and budget, we will say so. Our vinyl vs. Fibrex guide lays out the trade-offs.

In a climate this wet, how the window goes in matters as much as which window it is. We take care with the flashing, the sill, and the seal around every opening, and we finish the trim so it looks like it was always there.
